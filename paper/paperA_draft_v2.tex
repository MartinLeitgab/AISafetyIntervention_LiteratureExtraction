\begin{abstract}
Directories of AI safety research catalogue papers by metadata; embedding maps
place them by topical proximity. Neither records \emph{by what mechanism} a
proposed intervention is argued to reduce a specific risk. We apply a reasoning
LLM to 11{,}779 documents from the Alignment Research Dataset and extract, per
paper, the explicit logical chain running from a named risk through problem
analysis, theoretical insight, design rationale, implementation mechanism and
validation evidence to a proposed intervention, yielding 200{,}525 typed nodes
(19{,}096 risks, 36{,}959 interventions) in a queryable graph. On the
quality-controlled, de-duplicated set of 2{,}772 single-paper chains drawn from
1{,}868 documents, 87.4\% carry all five intermediate reasoning stages, and chain
length spans 4--16 nodes, indicating that the extraction captures argument
structure rather than filling a fixed template. We characterise the dataset with clustering-free descriptives --- most
notably an intervention maturity profile in which only 2.5\% of extracted
interventions are described as deployed --- and demonstrate mechanism-level
retrieval, in which a risk--intervention query returns the actual extracted
chain with its source citation. Finally, we report a set of measured failure
modes for graph-analytic inference on LLM-extracted safety corpora: we show that
near-duplicate merging manufactures a centrality super-hub whose eigenvector
centrality is 90$\times$ the next node, that excluding similarity edges does not
repair it, and that a keyword pattern occurring in 2.2\% of the corpus appears
in 2.2\% of the corpus can be made to look like a dominant pattern by conditioning
on a centrality-selected head. We release the extraction pipeline and the pathway
dataset.

\section{Introduction}

The AI safety literature grows faster than any individual can track, and the
question a practitioner actually asks of it is mechanistic: \emph{if I care
about risk $R$, what has been proposed to reduce it, by what mechanism, and how
far has that proposal been validated?} Existing resources cannot answer this.
Curated directories such as the Alignment Research Dataset (ARD) and Stampy
index papers by metadata. Embedding-based field maps place papers near
topically similar papers. In both cases the unit of organisation is the
document; the argument inside the document --- the chain from a risk to an
intervention --- is not represented, so it cannot be queried, aggregated, or
compared across papers.

This paper reports a dataset and a pipeline that represent that chain
explicitly. We prompt a reasoning LLM (\texttt{o3}) to read each paper and emit
a typed graph: \textsc{risk} and \textsc{intervention} endpoints connected
through five intermediate concept subtypes --- \emph{problem analysis},
\emph{theoretical insight}, \emph{design rationale}, \emph{implementation
mechanism}, \emph{validation evidence} --- with per-edge confidence and
per-intervention maturity and lifecycle attributes. Applied across the ARD
corpus, this produces a graph of 200{,}525 nodes over 11{,}779 documents.

\paragraph{Research question.} Can the mechanism by which proposed
interventions reduce AI safety risks be extracted at scale, with enough
structural fidelity to support mechanism-level querying and field-state
characterisation?

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{An extraction pipeline and its validation protocol} for reducing a
safety paper to the logical chain linking a risk to an intervention
(§Methods), with an LLM-as-judge faithfulness study and judge-of-judge
calibration.
\item \textbf{The pathway dataset}: 2{,}772 de-duplicated quality-controlled single-paper
risk$\rightarrow$intervention chains from 1{,}868 documents, of which 87.4\%
carry all five reasoning stages, released with the graph (§The Pathway Dataset).
\item \textbf{Clustering-free descriptive characterisation} of the extracted
field state --- chain-structure distribution, intervention maturity profile,
and end-to-end coverage --- none of which is derivable from paper metadata.
\item \textbf{A mechanism-level retrieval demonstration}: a risk--intervention
query returns the extracted chain and its citation, without any clustering step
(§Mechanism-Level Retrieval).
\item \textbf{Practical guidance for analysing LLM-extracted safety graphs}: four
measured failure modes, each with the control that removes it
(§Practical Guidance). We report these as measurements on this corpus, not as
folklore.
\end{enumerate}

We deliberately do \emph{not} claim ecosystem-level findings ("the field
neglects $X$"). We show in §Practical Guidance that the graph-analytic
quantities such claims are usually built from --- centrality rankings, cluster
risk-to-intervention ratios, co-occurrence on a centrality-selected head --- are
dominated on this kind of corpus by construction artefacts, and we treat that
result as a contribution rather than as a caveat.

\section{Related Work}
% [GAP: Axel started this section; needs prose + bibliography entries.
%  Four strands below are the agreed scope. ~3 paragraphs is the workshop bar.]

\paragraph{Knowledge graphs from scientific literature.}
% [CITE: SciERC / SemEval scientific IE; OpenIE over papers; CS-KG / ORKG.]
Prior work extracts entities and relations from scientific text, typically
targeting citation, method--task--dataset, or claim structures. Our schema
differs in that the relation of interest is an \emph{argument} internal to one
paper --- risk to intervention through explicit reasoning stages --- rather than
a bibliographic or method-level relation.

\paragraph{Agentic and self-organising knowledge graphs.}
% [CITE: Buehler 2025, "Agentic Deep Graph Reasoning Yields Self-Organizing
%  Knowledge Networks", arXiv:2502.13025 -- VERIFIED 2026-08-11.
%  Rasmussen et al. 2025, "Zep: A Temporal Knowledge Graph Architecture for Agent
%  Memory", arXiv:2501.13956 -- VERIFIED 2026-08-11.]
A parallel line of work has models build and extend knowledge graphs
autonomously: \citet{buehler2025agentic} shows a language model iteratively
growing a scale-free knowledge network through feedback, and
\citet{rasmussen2025zep} uses a temporal knowledge graph as agent memory. Those
systems optimise a graph for an agent's downstream reasoning; we optimise for
\emph{fidelity to what a specific paper argued}, which is why our edges are
confidence-scored assertions traceable to one source document rather than
model-generated links.

\paragraph{AI safety literature resources.}
% [CITE: Alignment Research Dataset; Stampy / AISafety.info; AI Safety Atlas;
%  MIT AI Risk Repository v2 (Slattery et al., Patterns 2026).]
ARD, Stampy and the AI Safety Atlas organise the field at the level of
documents or hand-curated topics. The MIT AI Risk Repository provides a curated
risk vocabulary. These are complementary to, not competitive with, a
mechanism-level extraction: a risk taxonomy says what can go wrong, whereas our
dataset records what has been proposed about it and on what argument.

\paragraph{Causal and mechanism extraction.}
% [CITE: causal claim extraction; intervention/outcome extraction in evidence-based
%  medicine (PICO); mechanism mining.]
Evidence-based medicine has a mature tradition of extracting
population--intervention--comparison--outcome structure from trials. Our task is
the analogous reduction for a field whose "evidence" is largely theoretical, which
is why validation evidence appears as a reasoning stage rather than as an outcome
measurement.

\paragraph{Graph-based retrieval-augmented generation.}
% All IDs below VERIFIED against the source 2026-08-11. Entries in refs.bib.
GraphRAG systems build a graph over a corpus so that a language model can retrieve
structured context rather than isolated passages
\citep{peng2024graphrag,sarmah2024hybridrag}, and the design space has been
mapped modularly \citep{cao2024legographrag} and pushed towards learning the graph
construction itself against downstream task reward
\citep{tsang2025autographr1}. The broader programme of joining language models to
knowledge graphs is surveyed by \citet{pan2024unifying}. The closest analogue to
our setting is domain-specific: \citet{wu2025medgraphrag} build a medical GraphRAG
whose selling point is that answers carry credible sourcing.

In all of that work the graph is \emph{instrumental} --- it exists to improve an
answer, and its edges are whatever the indexing step produced. Our graph is the
object of study rather than an index: each edge is a confidence-scored assertion
that one identified paper made, so a query returns that paper's own argument with
provenance attached rather than a synthesised answer. The two are complementary,
and a GraphRAG layer over this dataset is an obvious application we do not attempt
here.

\paragraph{LLM extraction validation.}
% [CITE: LLM-as-a-judge; judge-of-judge / meta-evaluation; faithfulness metrics.]
We adopt the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm with an explicit judge-of-judge calibration
step, and report its scope limits honestly in §Limitations.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Corpus and Extraction}

We use the Alignment Research Dataset, a curated collection of AI safety
publications spanning arXiv preprints, LessWrong and Alignment Forum posts, and
organisational reports. After filtering to documents that yield a parseable
extraction, the working corpus is 11{,}779 documents.
% SRC: experiment_query_demo_report.json -> coverage_bound.papers_with_risk = 11779

Each document is passed in full to \texttt{o3} with a schema-constrained
extraction prompt (Appendix~A). The model is instructed to trace the paper's own
logical chain and emit:

\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Concept nodes}, each tagged with one \texttt{concept\_category}
from \{\emph{risk}, \emph{problem analysis}, \emph{theoretical insight},
\emph{design rationale}, \emph{implementation mechanism}, \emph{validation
evidence}\}.
\item \textbf{Intervention nodes}, each carrying
\texttt{intervention\_lifecycle} $\in 1..6$ (position in the research-to-deployment
cycle) and \texttt{intervention\_maturity} $\in 1..4$ (1 = conceptual proposal,
2 = early prototype/experiment, 3 = mature method, 4 = deployed in practice).
\item \textbf{Typed edges} carrying a relation type, a natural-language
description of the claimed relationship, and \texttt{edge\_confidence} $\in 1..5$
reflecting how explicitly the paper asserts the link.
\end{itemize}

The canonical chain the schema encourages is
\emph{risk} $\rightarrow$ \emph{problem analysis} $\rightarrow$ \emph{theoretical
insight} $\rightarrow$ \emph{design rationale} $\rightarrow$ \emph{implementation
mechanism} $\rightarrow$ \emph{validation evidence} $\rightarrow$
\emph{intervention}. Papers frequently deviate from it --- omitting stages,
or supplying several nodes for one stage --- and we quantify that deviation in
§The Pathway Dataset rather than forcing conformity.

Per-paper graphs are embedded (1536-dimensional OpenAI embeddings over node name,
aliases and description) and loaded into FalkorDB, giving 200{,}525 nodes:
19{,}096 risks, 36{,}959 interventions, and 144{,}470 intermediate concept nodes
(27{,}748 problem analysis, 26{,}361 theoretical insight, 27{,}543 design
rationale, 34{,}222 implementation mechanism, 28{,}596 validation evidence).
% SRC: experiment_race_prevalence_report.json -> by_node_type_full_graph.*.n
%      (n per type; 19096+27748+26361+27543+34222+28596+36959 = 200525)

\paragraph{Corpus composition.} ARD is heterogeneous, and the chain dataset
inherits that: of the 2{,}772 chains, 1{,}055 come from arXiv, 326 from the
Alignment Forum, 267 from LessWrong, 265 from the EA Forum, 238 from YouTube
transcripts, 120 from MIRI, and the remainder from a long tail of institutional
and personal sites. A small number (7 chains, 0.3\%, from 3 documents) originate
from Google Docs, at least one of which is a reading list rather than a research
document --- a reminder that ``document'' in ARD does not always mean ``paper''.
% SRC: recomputed 2026-08-11 over paths_hopwise_v4_edge_only_deduped.jsonl,
%      grouping by the source URL host of each chain's risk node.

\paragraph{Extraction is single-source.} Every structural edge comes from one
paper. The global graph is a concatenation of per-paper graphs; the pipeline
never asserts a relation between concepts introduced by two different papers.
This is a deliberate fidelity choice and it has consequences for every
downstream graph statistic, which we make explicit below and in
§Practical Guidance.

\paragraph{Extraction failures, and the limits of automated repair.}
Extraction fails outright on a minority of ARD records. The large majority of
those failures are records with no usable source text --- ARD entries that carry
a title and URL but an empty or \texttt{None} body --- which are unrecoverable in
principle. Filtering to failures that are repairable in principle (a URL,
non-empty source text, and at least one extracted node) leaves \textbf{441
candidates}. Running the judge over all 441 in repair mode produced a non-empty
graph for \textbf{23 of them (5.2\%)}, and those recovered graphs are very thin
--- a mean of 2.4 nodes and 0.9 edges, against 16.0 nodes and 10.8 edges for the
audited successful extractions. The practical conclusion, which we state because
it is useful to others attempting the same thing: \emph{an LLM judge is effective
at auditing and repairing an extraction that already exists, and largely
ineffective at reconstructing one that failed.} We therefore treat failed
extractions as corpus loss rather than as a recoverable pool.

\subsection{Deduplication and Edge Normalisation}
% Reused near-verbatim from Gleb_overleaf_paper.txt L143-168.

The raw extraction produced duplicate nodes representing identical concepts with
different phrasings (e.g.\ ``US semiconductor export controls'' and ``AI chip
export control regulations'') and inconsistent edge types (e.g.\
\textit{mitigated\_by} vs \textit{mitigates}, naming variants like
\textit{design\_rationale} vs \textit{design\_rationale\_for}). We applied a
two-threshold deduplication: candidate pairs required both cosine similarity
$\geq 0.88$ on embeddings and Jaccard overlap $\geq 0.05$ on node names. We used
blocking by node type and category to reduce comparisons, FAISS
\texttt{IndexIVFFlat} for approximate similarity search, and Union-Find to group
transitive duplicates. For merged nodes we selected the canonical representative
by longest description and averaged embeddings. Four threshold configurations
were tested (Appendix~B). We prioritised precision over recall: false merges
corrupt downstream pathway analysis by conflating distinct concepts, whereas
missed duplicates only introduce redundancy. Edge types were normalised by
unifying bidirectional pairs (\textit{mitigates} $\rightarrow$
\textit{mitigated\_by}) and standardising naming variants, yielding 12 canonical
edge types. Deduplication removed 2{,}385 nodes (1.2\%).

\paragraph{Honest scope of the merge.} The merge is approximate in two respects
that we quantify rather than hide. First, the approximate nearest-neighbour
search under-retrieves: re-running the same threshold specification with
exhaustive search finds 54{,}282 candidate pairs against the 4{,}411 found by
the deployed configuration, a factor of 12.
% SRC: experiment_J6_merge_approx_v2_report.json -> variants.V2_T1_T2_T3.phase2_both_pass
%      = 54282 vs gleb_reference.candidate_pairs = 4411
Second, 1{,}140 nodes across 448 groups remain exact-name duplicates within
their category after the merge.
% SRC: experiment_J6_merge_approx_v2_report.json -> provenance_diagnostic
Both facts matter only where node identity is load-bearing; §Practical Guidance
shows exactly where that is, and the pathway results in
§The Pathway Dataset do not depend on the merge.

\subsection{Similarity Augmentation and Quality-Controlled Path Extraction}

Two graph layers are available for analysis, and the distinction governs every
claim in this paper.

\paragraph{EDGE layer (structural).} The extracted relations. Single-paper,
LLM-asserted, confidence-scored.

\paragraph{SIM layer (similarity).} Cosine-similarity edges between nodes of the
same concept category, added to connect content across documents. At
$\tau \geq 0.80$ this adds 1{,}435{,}806 within-category edges, reduces components
from 15{,}123 to 4{,}124, and expands the largest component from 61 nodes to
152{,}753 (76.2\%). That expansion is the point --- and also the hazard: a
threshold that turns 0.03\% of the graph into 76.2\% of it in one step is not a
neutral operation (Appendix~E).
% SRC: re-derived 2026-08-11 from graph_edge_data.pkl, SIMILARITY edges with
%      cos_sim >= 0.80 restricted to same concept_category.
%      NOTE: the frozen Overleaf reported 169,083 edges -> 6,522 components ->
%      142,772 (71.4%); that k-NN construction is ~8.5x sparser than the dump this
%      paper uses. Same qualitative conclusion, different counts; we report ours.

For every quantitative result in §The Pathway Dataset and
§Mechanism-Level Retrieval we use the \textbf{EDGE-only path set} under strict
quality cuts, applied at path-construction time: structural edges only (no
similarity hops), edge confidence $\geq 3$, intervention-endpoint maturity
$\geq 3$, exactly one risk node and it must be the path root. Enumerating all
such paths yields 8{,}954 raw chains.
% SRC: phase2_step4_F2v4_hopwise_falkordb.py (EDGE_CONFIDENCE_MIN=3,
%      INTERVENTION_MATURITY_MIN=3, single-risk); verified empirically in
%      experiment_paper_claim_audit.json: 100% of paths start at a risk node, 0 paths
%      contain a risk after position 0, all endpoints have maturity 3 (8050) or 4 (904).

\paragraph{De-duplication of near-identical chains within a paper (the reporting
unit).} Raw path enumeration over-counts prolific papers: a single paper
contributes 700 of the 8{,}954 raw chains (7.8\%), two others contribute 200 and
184, and 169 papers contribute ten or more. Reporting per-path statistics on that
set would weight the corpus by how branched each paper's extracted graph happens
to be --- the same unit-of-analysis error we document in §Practical Guidance. We
therefore collapse near-duplicate chains within each source paper: two chains are
near-duplicates when the smaller one's node set is $\geq 70\%$ contained in the
larger's, and the shorter is dropped. This yields the \textbf{2{,}772-chain
de-duplicated set} (at most 7 chains from any one paper), which is the reporting
unit for every number below. Both sets cover the same \textbf{1{,}868 source
papers}, and we report the raw set alongside as a sensitivity check.
% SRC: phase1_dedup_paths.py (CONTAINMENT_THRESHOLD = 0.70 -- note the module
%      docstring says 80%, the code uses 70%; the code is what ran).
%      Counts + skew: experiment_paper_claim_audit.json -> path_set_comparison.

The path set is free of similarity-threshold sensitivity and of merge artefacts
by construction; it is the substrate on which we are willing to make numerical
claims.

\subsection{Validation}

Extraction quality is assessed with an LLM-as-a-judge protocol. A judge model
from a different provider than the extractor (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-5}, batch
API) receives the source text together with the extracted graph and returns a
structured audit: schema conformance, referential integrity, orphan and duplicate
detection, rationale consistency, coverage of the source's claims, and a set of
proposed repairs. We ran it on a random sample of \textbf{100 successfully
extracted papers} spanning all nine ARD source types (LessWrong 28, Alignment
Forum 21, blogs 15, EA Forum 11, arXiv 11, Arbital 6, special documents 4,
YouTube transcripts 2, aisafety.info 2). A judge-of-judge stage then had three
independent meta-graders (\texttt{claude-opus-4-5}, \texttt{gemini-3-pro},
\texttt{gpt-5.1}) score each extraction before and after the judge's proposed
repairs.
% SRC: experiment_judge_item2_report.json (n=100, by_source_type)
%      + extraction_validator/STATUS.md on branch judge_handoff_workshop_items_2_3

\paragraph{Structural error classes.} Across the 100 papers the judge found
\textbf{108 referential-integrity findings}, of which 68 are blocker-severity and
the dominant pattern is an edge whose source or target names a node absent from
the node list; \textbf{42 orphan nodes} (present in 40\% of papers); and
\textbf{56 within-paper duplicate node pairs} (55\% of papers). The mean audited
graph is 16.0 nodes and 10.8 edges.
% SRC: experiment_judge_item2_report.json -> findings_total,
%      findings_papers_with_at_least_one, per_paper_means

\paragraph{Coverage.} Asked to identify content in the source that the extraction
missed, the judge proposed \textbf{new nodes for only 7 of 100 papers} (9 nodes
in total). Its coverage list flags a mean of 7.8 relationships per paper that it
judges the source to support but the extraction not to contain --- an
upper-bound opinion signal rather than a measured omission rate, since the judge
has no schema slot in which to propose those edges and they were never
adjudicated.

agreement that the judge improved the extraction is unanimous. They disagree

\paragraph{What the post-repair score is.} The meta-graders were shown the source
text, the extraction, and the judge's report including its \emph{proposed} fixes,
and asked to score the extraction before and after those fixes. The post-repair
score is therefore an assessment of the proposed repairs as described, not a
measurement of a re-built graph. This is a real limitation --- no repaired graph
was re-extracted and re-scored --- but it also means the known defects in the
fix-application code (which under-applied multi-node additions and dropped
proposed merges) do \emph{not} contaminate these scores.

Across the 95 papers it scored, it recorded \textbf{216 missed concepts}

(in 16\% of papers) and \textbf{15 category errors} (in 33\% of papers) --- i.e.\
under this grader the dominant failure mode is omission, not hallucination.
These counts are un-adjudicated LLM output and are reported as a profile, not as
a measured error rate; the manual taxonomy remains outstanding.
% SRC: experiment_judge_full_report.json -> item3_error_taxonomy_auto

\paragraph{Two measurement caveats we do not hide.}
First, the judge grades against a schema that expects rationale fields inline on
nodes and edges, whereas the pipeline stores rationale as separate nodes linked
by a dedicated relation. That mismatch accounts for \textbf{184 of the 218
blocker-severity schema flags (84\%)}; excluding it leaves 34 substantive
blocker-severity schema findings across 100 papers. Any headline "error rate"
computed from the raw judge output without this split is measuring a schema
version difference, not extraction quality.
% SRC: experiment_judge_item2_report.json -> schema_check_severity_rationale_field_mismatch
%      vs schema_check_severity_substantive
Second, the judge scores node and edge faithfulness, referential structure and
edge type. It does \emph{not} score the \texttt{intervention\_maturity}
assignment. Wherever maturity appears below it is labelled LLM-assessed and used
descriptively, never as a validated measurement.

\section{The Pathway Dataset}

This section characterises what the extraction produced. Every number is a
structural property of the EDGE-only quality-cut path set or a direct count over
extracted attributes. No clustering, no centrality, no similarity threshold
enters any of them.

\subsection{Chains carry the full reasoning structure}

Across the 2{,}772 de-duplicated chains, \textbf{87.4\% (2{,}423) contain all
five intermediate reasoning stages}. Stage-wise presence is: problem analysis
99.0\%, theoretical insight 93.9\%, design rationale 96.1\%, implementation
mechanism 96.0\%, validation evidence 98.0\%. On the raw 8{,}954-chain set the
same figure is 89.0\%, so the result is not an artefact of the de-duplication
choice.
% SRC: experiment_paper_claim_audit.json -> path_set_comparison.deduped
%      (all5 2423/2772 = 87.4%; subtype_presence_pct) and .raw (89.0%)

This is the central property of the dataset: a large majority of extracted
chains do not merely connect a risk to an intervention, they carry the
paper's argument for why the intervention should work, stage by stage.

\paragraph{What this is not.} 87.4\% is the share \emph{of complete chains} that
carry all five stages, not the share of the corpus. Only 1{,}868 of the 11{,}779
documents (15.9\%) yield any complete high-confidence chain at all; the
remaining documents contribute nodes and partial structure. Both numbers matter:
the corpus-level yield is modest, and what is yielded is structurally rich.

\subsection{The chains are not a filled-in template}

produce seven-node chains regardless of the source. The length distribution

span 4--16 nodes. The mode is indeed 7 (1{,}775 chains, 64.0\%), but 25.8\%
of chains are \emph{longer} than the canonical seven --- carrying multiple nodes
for one or more stages, i.e.\ richer argumentation --- and 10.2\% are shorter,
carrying genuine gaps.

\section{Practical Guidance for Analysing LLM-Extracted Safety Graphs}

A graph of this shape invites standard network analysis: cluster the nodes, rank
by centrality, compare risk and intervention densities per cluster, look for
co-occurring framings. We ran that analysis. This section reports what we
measured when we checked those quantities against construction artefacts, because
the failure modes are systematic, reproducible, and --- in our experience ---
not obvious in advance. Each is stated as a measurement on this corpus with the
control that removes it.

\subsection{Near-duplicate merging manufactures a centrality super-hub}

The corpus contains thousands of near-identical existential-risk concept nodes
(``Existential catastrophe from misaligned advanced AI'' and close paraphrases)
contributed by different papers. Merging them is the natural cleanup step, and
it is exactly what breaks centrality: the canonical node inherits every member's
structural edges. In our measurement the largest merged risk node absorbed
\textbf{4{,}066 members and 7{,}777 structural edges}.
% SRC: experiment_merge_vs_simexcl_ec_report.json -> biggest_merged_node_members / _edge_degree

Table~\ref{tab:ec} shows eigenvector centrality under the four combinations of
(merged / un-merged) $\times$ (similarity edges included / risk--risk similarity
excluded).

Two consequences follow, and their order matters. \textbf{(a)~Un-merging is
primary.} On the merged graph the super-hub retains a 90$\times$ lead even after
all risk--risk similarity edges are deleted, because the merge baked the
concentration into the structural topology. \textbf{(b)~Excluding risk--risk
similarity is still necessary}, but as the \emph{second} step: on the un-merged
graph it flattens the top centrality value by a factor of 31 (0.0328
$\rightarrow$ 0.0011) by breaking the near-duplicate similarity clique. Applied
alone, either control leaves the artefact intact.

The substantive point for readers of this literature: \emph{``the most central
risks are existential'' is a statement about graph construction, not about the
field.} On the un-merged graph the ten highest-centrality nodes differ from each
other by less than 1\%, i.e.\ there is no ranking to report.

\paragraph{Control.} Do not merge near-duplicates before computing centrality;
exclude within-category similarity edges from all centrality measures; count
canonical concepts or source papers, never raw nodes, for any co-occurrence
statistic.

\subsection{Approximate near-duplicate search under-removes, unpredictably}

Deduplication quality is usually reported as a removal rate. That number is
uninformative on its own: re-running our own threshold specification with
exhaustive rather than approximate search returns \textbf{54{,}282 candidate
pairs against 4{,}411} --- a factor of 12 --- and the gap is concentrated almost
entirely in the risk block, precisely where the centrality artefact lives.
% SRC: experiment_J6_merge_approx_v2_report.json -> variants.V2_T1_T2_T3 vs gleb_reference

\paragraph{Control.} Report recall of the candidate-generation stage, not only
the removal rate; verify on the node block that downstream metrics are most
sensitive to.

\subsection{Loose similarity thresholds group topics, not mechanisms}

Direct $k$-means on the 1536-dimensional embeddings fails outright (silhouette
$\approx 0.02$). UMAP reduction to 150 dimensions followed by $k$-means at
$k{=}40$ reaches silhouette 0.298 (Appendix~C), and the resulting clusters are
recognisable field topics (Appendix~D). But cluster co-membership at
$\tau{=}0.80$ tracks vocabulary, not mechanism: the same threshold that produces
navigable topics also merges the largest connected component from 61 nodes to
152{,}753. We attempted to derive a clean, non-overlapping risk/intervention
taxonomy this way, tightening quality cuts step by step --- maturity, edge
confidence, EDGE-only paths, clustering each node type on its own axis --- and
node-level semantic clustering remained insufficient to carry mechanism-level
conclusions.

\paragraph{Control.} Treat node-level clusters as a browsing index, not as an
inferential unit. Cluster risk and intervention nodes on separate axes. Do not
compute risk-to-intervention ratios per cluster: the numerator and denominator
are populated by different extraction stages and the ratio inherits the merge
artefact above.

\subsection{Co-occurrence measured on a centrality-selected head is circular}

Selecting the top-100 risks by eigenvector centrality and measuring which
framings appear there is a natural exploratory move, and in an earlier pass on
this corpus it produced a striking-looking result: 88\% of the single-path risks
in that head appeared to be framed through competitive race dynamics, with an
apparent 44-fold gradient from the centrality head to the full risk population.
Neither figure survives re-derivation. Re-running the same measurement under the
same definitions --- importance by eigenvector centrality, path diversity by
distinct first-hop structural problem-analysis neighbours, race framing by the
same keyword rule --- reproduces the \emph{structural} part (38 of the top-100
risks are single-path, against 41 reported) but not the framing part: 2.6\% of
those single-path risks are race-framed, and the head-to-population gradient is
2.0-fold, not 44-fold.
% SRC: experiment_race_top100_rederive_report.json -> condition_A (merged + full
%      within-category SIM, the closest reconstruction of the original setup).
%      The similarity layer in the graph dump we use is ~8.5x denser than the one
%      the original analysis ran on, so the centrality ranking -- and hence the
%      selected head -- is not identical. We report the failure to reproduce rather
%      than the original figure.
The corpus-wide prevalence of the framing is \textbf{2.21\% of problem-analysis
nodes} (614/27{,}748) and 1.65\% of risk nodes; only 0.50\% of EDGE-only chains
contain a race-framed problem-analysis node, and the re-derived head rates
(1.3--6.3\% across centrality tiers) sit within a small multiple of that base.
% SRC: experiment_race_prevalence_report.json + experiment_race_top100_rederive_report.json

\paragraph{The lesson is stronger, not weaker, for the reproduction failing.}
A selection-conditioned statistic on this kind of graph is not merely inflated,
it is \emph{unstable}: it depends on the similarity-layer density and the merge
decisions that set the ranking, and those are pipeline parameters rather than
properties of the literature. A number computed this way should not be reported
at all without the selection rule, the graph construction, and a full-population
baseline stated alongside it.

The underlying association is nevertheless real but modest. Table~\ref{tab:race}
shows the race$\times$existential-risk co-occurrence odds ratio under three

supports no claim about the field's discourse structure.

corrected merge, the same deletion isolates \textbf{2 of 100}.

\paragraph{Control.} Report prevalence on the full population before reporting
any pattern in a selected head; state the selection rule as part of the result;
choose the unit of analysis (node / merged node / paper) explicitly and show that
the effect survives all three.

\subsection{Single-source extraction makes several ``findings'' true by design}

Three quantities that look like discoveries are consequences of the pipeline.
\emph{Path diversity}: because each paper contributes one chain, a risk
discussed in one paper necessarily has one route to an intervention --- this is
extraction structure, not fragility in the literature. \emph{Path completion}:
under risk-rooted breadth-first search over a similarity-augmented graph, most
branches terminate at intermediate stages, so a low completion percentage
measures the traversal, not the corpus. \emph{Siloing}: cross-paper links exist
only in the similarity layer, so the structural layer is siloed by construction
while the similarity layer's connectivity is a function of $\tau$; no layer
measures real siloing.

\paragraph{Control.} Before reporting any structural statistic, ask what value it
would take on a corpus of independent single-paper chains. If the answer is
``the value we observed'', it is a pipeline property.

\section{Limitations and Outlook}

\paragraph{Corpus.} ARD is a curated snapshot and its coverage thins after 2023;
frontier-era discourse is under-represented. Claims here describe the extracted
corpus, not the field as of publication.

\paragraph{Single extractor.} All extractions come from one model
(\texttt{o3}) under one prompt. Model-specific extraction bias is not
characterised by our validation design.
% [GAP: Mike ran an n=20 multi-model consistency check (o3 / GPT-5 / Claude-4).
%  If those numbers are recoverable, one sentence here materially strengthens this
%  limitation. Owner: Sai/Mike via ticket #147 recovery bundle.]

\paragraph{Validation is LLM-based.} Faithfulness is judged by an LLM with
judge-of-judge calibration, not by domain experts at scale.

\paragraph{LLM-assigned attributes.} Concept subtype, edge confidence,
intervention lifecycle and maturity are model assignments. Only node/edge
faithfulness and edge type are scored by the judge; maturity in particular is
descriptive here.

\paragraph{Outlook.} The natural next step, and the reason the dataset is
released, is cross-paper mechanism analysis: grouping interventions into
mechanism families and anchoring risks to an external risk taxonomy, so that one
can ask which mechanism families address which risks, at what stage of validation
evidence, across the whole corpus --- and, where a mechanism family is densely
populated for every risk but one, whether that gap is a research opportunity.
We deliberately do not attempt it here: §Practical Guidance shows that
node-level semantic clustering on this data is not strong enough to carry
mechanism-family grouping, and doing it properly requires an external anchor
rather than a better threshold.

\section{Conclusion}

We extracted, from 11{,}779 AI safety documents, the explicit logical chains by
which proposed interventions are argued to reduce named risks, and showed that
87.4\% of the 2{,}772 quality-controlled chains carry all five reasoning stages
while spanning 4--16 nodes in length --- structure that is captured from the papers
rather than imposed on them. The resulting dataset answers mechanism-level
queries that document-level resources cannot, and characterises the field's
intervention maturity profile, in which 2.5\% of extracted interventions are
described as deployed. We also report, as a first-class result, four measured
ways in which routine graph analysis of such a corpus produces artefacts rather
than findings --- most sharply, a merge-manufactured centrality hub at 90$\times$
the next node --- together with the controls that remove them. We hope both the
dataset and the guidance are useful to teams building on LLM-extracted
scientific graphs.

\section{Appendix E: Similarity Threshold and Hop Sensitivity}
% Reused from Gleb_overleaf_paper.txt Appendices G + H, merged.

\paragraph{Similarity threshold ($\tau$).}
We tested $\tau \in \{0.80, 0.85, 0.90, 0.95\}$ on within-category node pairs.
At $\tau{=}0.80$, 169{,}083 similarity edges connect conceptually related nodes
across documents. Higher thresholds progressively fragment the graph:
$\tau{=}0.85$ reduces edges by approximately 50\%, $\tau{=}0.90$ by over 90\%,
and $\tau{=}0.95$ captures only near-duplicate phrasings. The main text uses
EDGE-only paths for all quantitative claims precisely because of this
sensitivity.

\paragraph{PageRank mixing coefficient ($\alpha$).}
For the centrality analyses in §Practical Guidance we combine structural and
similarity transition matrices as
$P = \alpha P_{\text{struct}} + (1-\alpha) P_{\text{sim}}$.
Rankings are stable across $\alpha \in [0.5, 0.9]$ (Spearman $\rho = 1.0$,
identical top-20); only pure structural weighting ($\alpha = 1.0$) deviates, with
2 risks leaving the top-20. Note that this stability is a robustness property of
the mixing parameter and says nothing about the merge artefact documented in
§Practical Guidance --- the rankings are stably contaminated.

\section{Appendix F: Race-Framing Classifier Validation}
% Reused from Gleb_overleaf_paper.txt Appendix J, kept because it is a valid
% classifier validation; prevalence claims moved to the main text.

We validated keyword-based race-framing classification (keywords: ``competi-'',
``race'') on a stratified sample of 52 problem-analysis nodes.
\textbf{Stratum 1} ($n{=}36$): all race-flagged PA nodes from top-100
single-path risks --- 36/36 = 100\% precision. Example PAs: ``Competitive
deployment pressures reducing safety measures,'' ``AI arms race dynamics
reducing safety investment.'' \textbf{Stratum 2} ($n{=}16$): random sample from
remaining risks --- 12/16 = 75\% precision. The four false positives matched
keywords but described competition \emph{among} AI systems rather than a human
race \emph{to develop} AI. Overall precision 48/52 = 92\%.

\paragraph{Recall estimation.} On a stratified sample ($n{=}50$) of non-flagged
PA nodes, one false negative was found among top-100 single-path risks
(``AI developers racing without adequate safety''), giving recall 36/37 = 97.3\%
in that stratum. Recall falls to an estimated $\sim$68\% in tiers 101--500 due to
morphological variants and synonyms without keywords.

\paragraph{Scope.} This validates the \emph{classifier} on the sampled strata. It
establishes nothing about prevalence: corpus-wide prevalence is reported in
§Practical Guidance (2.21\% of problem-analysis nodes) and was measured
separately by exhaustive scan.

\section{Appendix G: A Failure Case}

Quality cuts do not make every extracted chain meaningful. The chain below passes
every structural filter we apply --- seven nodes, all five reasoning stages, edge
confidence $\geq 3$, a maturity-4 intervention endpoint, single source --- and is
nonetheless spurious. Its source is an expository mathematics article proving
that there are infinitely many primes.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{risk} Prime scarcity affecting cryptographic key space $\rightarrow$
\textbf{pa} Finite prime assumption in key generation algorithms $\rightarrow$
\textbf{ti} Contradiction from finite prime list assumption $\rightarrow$
\textbf{dr} Product-plus-one construction to find new prime $\rightarrow$
\textbf{im} Compute $P = \prod(\text{all known primes}) + 1$ $\rightarrow$
\textbf{va} Proof that $P$ has a prime divisor outside the original list
$\rightarrow$ \textbf{intv} Integrate infinite-prime theorem into cryptographic
key generation standards
\end{quote}
% SRC: node ids [60117..60123], source https://arbital.com/p/infinitely_many_primes

The extraction has taken Euclid's proof and cast it into the risk--intervention
schema, inventing a safety framing ("prime scarcity") the source never asserts.
This is the characteristic failure mode of schema-guided extraction over a
heterogeneous corpus: when a document contains a problem, an argument and a
construction, the schema will find a chain whether or not the document is about
safety at all. It is also why the structural statistics in §The Pathway Dataset
are reported as structural properties and never as evidence that a chain is
substantively correct --- that is what the judge study (§Methods) is for.
